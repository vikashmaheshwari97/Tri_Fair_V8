What MO-CAPO actually demonstrated

Uploaded source: MO-CAPO: Multi-Objective Cost-Aware Prompt Optimization, arXiv:2605.18869v1, Sections 3.2, 5, 6 and Appendix A.7. Their claim concerns efficiently discovered accuracy-cost trade-off sets, with competitive maximum accuracy. Their study uses three models, four datasets, three seeds, and 7.5M downstream tokens per run.

  • Noisy R2: best mean in 8/12 settings; NSGA-II leads 3, initial instructions lead 1 (Table 2).
  • Optimistic HV: highest in 8/12 (Section 6.1).
  • Pessimistic HV: highest mean in 6/12, counted from Table 2. It is not best everywhere.
  • Maximum-accuracy representative: best among all methods in 2/12 settings, with the representative selected using development quality and then scored on test (Table 3).
  • MO-CAPO pessimistic HV exceeds NSGA-II optimistic HV in 5/12 settings; reverse occurs once. This is stronger than merely comparing optimistic HV.
  • The paper reports up to 5.7 times more evaluated candidates and up to 88% lower budget to complete the first iteration. These efficiency findings require comparable token trajectories; they are not claims that every deployed prompt is cheapest.

The original paper is two-objective and uses shared data-derived min-max bounds. Tri-Fair is three-objective with fixed study bounds; the protocol must state this difference. HV values from the two studies are not numerically comparable.

Writing the experimental results

  1. Approximation-set quality: held-out noisy R2, optimistic HV, pessimistic HV and their gap; mean and sample standard deviation across independent seeds.
  2. Convergence and evaluation efficiency: real token checkpoints, candidates measured, blocks per candidate, budget to first improved front, and time-to-target.
  3. Three-objective trade-offs: show actual prompt vectors; state whether a representative is balanced, quality-first, cost-first or fairness-first.
  4. Maximum-accuracy comparison: choose using development/validation quality and report untouched test performance, preserving that prompt's cost and unfairness.
  5. Ablation and sensitivity: isolate progressive evaluation, failure feedback, demonstration search, and operator selection. v8.3 versus the hybrid changes several components and is not a clean single-component ablation.
  6. Robustness: group support, fairness uncertainty, paired gender consistency, independent seeds and a prespecified analysis.
